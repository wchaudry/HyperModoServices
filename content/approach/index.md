## Your people own it

Consulting engagements end one of two ways. Either the client depends on the firm indefinitely — every change, every incident, every new feed goes back through the consultants — or the firm leaves, and the expertise leaves with it, hollowing out the organization it was hired to help.

I have watched both happen, and I have built the alternative. At Zones LLC the platform was delivered by a seventy-person internal organization recruited and trained for it, most of them through a graduate program designed so the company would own its own capability. The measure of a successful engagement is not that the client calls me back; it is that they don't have to.

So every practice area carries two deliverables: the thing itself, and the people on your side who own it afterwards.

## A deliverable at every step

Nothing here ends in a recommendation and a handshake. Each stage produces something you keep:

- **Business case** — market sizing, competitive analysis, business model, vision, SWOT, phased roadmap, and the presentation to the executive team.
- **Design** — journey maps, user flows, Figma mockups validated against real statements of work.
- **Architecture** — the data model with lineage, the ingestion and integration design, infrastructure as code.
- **Test plan** — sized in hours per case, not adjectives; derived from requirements written ahead of the build.
- **Readiness assessment** — what is built, what is validated, what still has to happen before go-live, and who owns each item.
- **Cutover and handover** — support model, runbooks, cross-training, and a named successor for every role I held.

## Agents under a framework

For the past year most of the code on a live production system has been written by AI agents, with me directing and reviewing. That works because of the operating model around it, not the prompting:

- a written definition of what a defect is — a specific input or state that produces a wrong result, lost data, or a false statement;
- a review loop that runs until a round raises nothing, not until the findings seem reasonable;
- persistent memory across sessions, so nothing is re-investigated and every correction becomes a rule the next session inherits;
- gates enforced by hooks in the repository, not by asking nicely.

I install this in your repositories and train your engineers to direct and review agent-written code. It is a toolkit; installing it takes a day.

## How an engagement runs

1. **A first conversation** — what you are trying to reach in the market, what stands in the way, and whether I am the right person. Thirty minutes.
2. **A scoped proposal** — the deliverables, who on your side owns each afterwards, the timeline, the price. Fixed fee or time and materials, corp-to-corp through Xymbia Inc.
3. **The work** — weekly written status that names root causes and slips as they happen, not after.
4. **Handover** — planned from day one. The engagement ends when your team is running it.

Two to six weeks to start. Full programs, fractional roles, or a single deliverable.
